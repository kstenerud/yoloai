Can a foreign netdev chain on the same device shadow ours?

-- controls and baselines (all held, or this would not render) --
  baseline: the guest reaches the denied host: True   (no chain at all; without this every block is free)
  our deny fired: True   (2 packets)
  the foreign chain really is evaluating traffic: True   (2 packets — without this the foreign chain is inert and its failure to shadow proves nothing)
  still blocked with a foreign DROP in front: True
  the foreign drop fired: True   (2 packets)

-- measurements --
  ours only: False
  a SECOND netdev ingress chain attaches to the same device: True   (if the kernel refuses this, the whole shadowing class is closed at the door)
  still blocked with a foreign higher-priority ACCEPT in front [arm: shadow]: True
  and our deny still counted, so it was still reached [arm: shadow]: True   (2 -> 4)
  a foreign terminal DROP stops our counter advancing [arm: shadow]: True   (ours 4 -> 4. If masked, a counter-based detector reads zero on a sandbox whose traffic is being dropped by someone else — enforcement intact, signature identical to inert)
  so a counter-based detector would call that sandbox: 'INERT (wrongly)'   (the reason V1b's subscription design is worth having: it never infers enforcement from a counter at all)

-- expectations, rendered from the values above --
  [PASS] our chain blocks, alone on the device  <- ours only=False
  [PASS] a foreign accept at higher priority does NOT shadow our deny  <- still blocked with a foreign higher-priority ACCEPT in front=True

-- UNBASELINED CLAIMS, resting on a probe never shown to report failure --
  a foreign accept at higher priority does NOT shadow our deny: the baseline is the no-chain state above; this arm adds a chain rather than removing the mechanism, so the discriminating before/after is 'ours only' vs 'ours plus foreign', both recorded

-- WHAT WAS NOT TRIED --
  - the EGRESS hook. Every chain here is ingress; whether egress base chains interact differently is unasked
  - a foreign chain installed by something real — firewalld, a CNI plugin, another container runtime. This is a hand-built adversary and the shapes real software installs may differ
  - priority collisions: two chains at the SAME priority on one device, where ordering is unspecified and a design cannot rely on it
  - whether a foreign chain can be installed by anything less privileged than root, which decides whether this is an attack or only an accident
  - the nftables `netdev` egress hook, `flowtable` offload, and XDP, all of which sit at or before ingress and none of which is examined
  - containers. Hand-built veth, per R11's reasoning
  - whether OUR chain could equally shadow someone else's, which is the same question pointed at the blast radius of shipping this
